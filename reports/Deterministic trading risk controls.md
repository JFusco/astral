# Deterministic controls make paper trading testable

**ASTRAL should pursue net profit through reproducible strategies constrained by a separate risk authority and one recoverable executor.** Determinism can make arithmetic and order handling accurate; it cannot make a market forecast correct. The implementation produces auditable research proposals and applies hard execution vetoes, while preserving every order outcome. Swing and intraday experiments need separate attribution but share account cash, reservations, concentration limits, and loss circuits. This report uses primary-source research checked **October 24, 2026**. User-saved local paper credentials now support authenticated Alpaca REST readback, and the first small broker-paper BTC entry and exit both filled. **42 local tests have passed**; this local fault/arithmetic evidence is separate from the completed provider-paper lifecycle. Autonomous strategies, broker protection, live orders, and hosted 24/7 operation remain unavailable or unimplemented.

## Repeatable proposals expose assumptions before risking capital

Use pure numerical functions for signal calculation, sizing, and risk decisions. Pass decision time, prices, account state, instrument metadata, and policy explicitly; record their versions and hashes. Decimal arithmetic constructed from serialized decimal values avoids binary floating-point surprises, but still requires finite-value checks, sufficient precision, and deliberate rounding. The same canonical input should reproduce the same proposal. Neither news prose nor an LLM response should authorize an order or change a hard limit. ([Python Decimal](https://docs.python.org/3.13/library/decimal.html))

The implemented [proposal module](../backend/risk.py) uses **ATR14 with Wilder smoothing**, a moving-average trend filter, and a breakout above the previous 20 bars. Swing proposals use daily bars and SMA20/SMA50; intraday proposals use five-minute bars and SMA10/SMA30. A candidate requires the latest close above the fast average, the fast average above the slow, and the close above the prior breakout reference. A close below the slow average produces an exit-review flag; otherwise the result is hold. These are frozen research hypotheses, not validated trading edges. Their present purpose is to generate explainable candidates without discretionary model arithmetic.

True range is the maximum of the bar's high-low range and the absolute distances from high and low to the previous close. The module seeds ATR with the mean of 14 true ranges and then applies `(13 × previous_ATR + new_TR) / 14`. ATR measures price movement, not direction. Choosing 14 periods or an ATR multiplier is a policy choice, not evidence that the resulting stop or signal is optimal. ([Fidelity ATR guide](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/technical-analysis/technical-indicator-guide/atr))

Current proposals place their planning stop reference two ATR below entry for swing and 1.5 ATR below entry for intraday. The accompanying target reference is two stop distances above entry. The module subtracts modeled round-trip fees and slippage from target reward and requires net reward divided by stressed loss to be at least one before labeling an entry candidate. This is an arbitrary economic-feasibility hurdle, not an expected-return estimate. **Neither reference is an order, a maximum-loss guarantee, or proof of positive expected return.** A stop can execute far from its trigger; a stop-limit can remain unfilled. Even a favorable modeled reward/loss ratio supplies no winning probability. Under a simplified two-outcome model, break-even probability is `loss / (net_reward + loss)`; a credible expected-return estimate still needs unseen observations and realistic costs. ([FINRA stop-order risks](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/stop-orders-factors-consider-during-volatile-markets))

The current `/api/risk/plan` route accepts user-supplied historical bars and requires connected account and quote information. It records research results with execution disabled; the server's current trade gate can still veto a candidate. Bar provenance supplied by a caller is a claim requiring verification. Automated acquisition, calendar-complete coverage, independently verified history, a protective-order lifecycle, and an autonomous strategy scheduler must precede unattended strategy execution. Local arithmetic tests do not replace those contracts. Protective stops have not been executed, and live orders are unavailable.

## Shared capital limits matter more than the signal's confidence

Size from an explicit planned-loss budget rather than from enthusiasm, recent wins, or an AI confidence score. CME describes the relationship between account risk allowance, stop distance, and position size, while explicitly calling its illustrative 2% threshold arbitrary. The useful principle is budgeting loss; the particular percentage needs a declared experiment and personal tolerance. ([CME position sizing](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/trade-and-risk-management/proper-position-size), [CME's arbitrary threshold](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/trade-and-risk-management/the-2-percent-rule))

For equity `E`, risk fraction `f`, entry reference `P`, and planned stop `S`, estimate loss per unit as `P − S + entry_cost + exit_cost + adverse_slippage + gap_stress`. Divide `E × f` by that estimate, then constrain quantity by affordable cash including entry fees, order notional, symbol/group/gross headroom, and valid instrument increments. Round quantity down to the actual increment and recalculate every limit. A `0.05` increment requires multiples of `0.05`, not merely two decimal places. Reject a below-minimum result. Wider stops or higher assumed costs must never increase size when other inputs are unchanged.

The current proposal budgets are **0.25% of equity for swing and 0.10% for intraday**. Its explicit stress assumptions add 25 basis points of crypto fees per side, 10 basis points adverse slippage, and a further 1% ETF or 2% crypto gap allowance. These are paper experiment defaults, not statistically established loss bounds. The existing executor additionally limits planned order notional to **$1,000**, gross exposure to **50%**, individual ETFs to **10%**, individual crypto to **5%**, and economic groups to **25%**. Market orders have a stricter **$100 planned-notional ceiling**. All values require review before eventual live use; none guarantees the realized fill or loss remains inside its planning estimate.

The proposal function currently limits by its loss budget, cash, and supplied notional cap; server execution checks provide additional vetoes. **Aggregate planned-stop risk across existing positions is not yet a complete allocation system.** Before strategy automation, pending entries must consume an aggregate loss allowance, and holdings with unknown stop plans must not count as zero risk. Keep separate swing/intraday inventories and deterministic fill attribution so an intraday exit cannot accidentally liquidate the swing allocation. Multiple brokers need venue-specific cash limits and a combined economic-exposure view; additional accounts do not create additional global capital.

Static groups are a transparent first approximation to concentration. SPY and QQQ can overlap; direct crypto and a crypto ETF can represent the same underlying exposure. FINRA specifically identifies correlated holdings and overlapping funds as concentration risks. Add aligned rolling-correlation diagnostics later, but do not let an estimated negative correlation automatically unlock leverage or larger positions. Stress simultaneous adverse moves and higher costs separately. Volatility sizing is a risk policy: empirical research does not establish that volatility-managed equity strategies systematically improve out-of-sample performance. ([FINRA concentration risk](https://syndication.finra.org/content/concentrate-concentration-risk-0), [Cederburg and colleagues](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf))

## Execution must survive ambiguity, gaps, and process failure

Default new entries to a bounded limit using a fresh bid/ask quote. A patient signal can use a passive limit with expiry; an urgent but price-sensitive signal can use a marketable limit. A market order prioritizes execution while accepting uncertain price. A limit controls acceptable price while accepting nonexecution. Bound repricing attempts, cumulative tolerance, lifetime, and signal expiry; do not silently convert an unsuccessful limit to market. Stops and stop-limits address different risks and cannot promise an exit during a gap or outage. ([SEC trading basics](https://www.sec.gov/files/trading101basics.pdf))

Verify account status, securities/crypto permissions, broker restrictions, instrument tradability, current increments, quantity type, session, and supported order combinations. Fractional ETFs and crypto have different time-in-force constraints; generic equity bracket semantics must not be assumed for crypto. Crypto's seven-day schedule does not authorize ETF orders overnight. Keep the initial policy long-only and cash-funded even when a broker reports margin buying power. Do not encode obsolete Alpaca PDT restrictions: Alpaca reports migrating to the replacement intraday margin framework in June 2026. Another broker can follow a different transition schedule, and current account readback still governs. ([Alpaca orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca), [Crypto order capabilities](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/crypto-orders), [Alpaca intraday margin migration](https://alpaca.markets/blog/finra-retires-the-pdt-rule-introducing-alpacas-new-intraday-margin-framework/))

Persist intent and a stable client-order identifier before submission. **A network timeout means unknown outcome, not permission to resend.** Retain cash and exposure reservations and reconcile using the original identity; a temporary missing lookup does not prove absence. A cancellation request likewise does not release capacity until terminal broker evidence arrives. Deduplicate cumulative partial fills, including fills during pending cancellation, and refresh account positions before releasing reservations. Startup must bind the journal to its paper account and recover external holdings and working orders. Alpaca explicitly warns that timed-out submissions can have reached the market. ([Alpaca timeout guidance](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/working-with-orders))

Persist daily equity baselines and high-water equity. The current **1% daily-loss and 5% high-water-drawdown circuits latch across restart and require operator review**; midnight does not automatically erase the cause. Define the account boundary and adjust for external cash flows before interpreting equity change as trading profit. Halting new entries, canceling working entries, and reducing positions are distinct actions. An entry halt does not prove a flat account. Reducing sells still need verified holdings, broker eligibility, and the configured execution policy.

Run quote, reconciliation, cancellation, and risk loops independently of article processing and model work. Reserve API capacity for existing risk. Around-the-clock operation needs a supervisor, external heartbeat monitor, recoverable storage/backups, and alerts that still work when the trading process crashes. FINRA's algorithmic guidance supports limited pilots, version control, adverse-market testing, reconciliation, and rapid disable capability as control patterns. These are useful engineering precedents for this personal system, not a claim that it satisfies a member firm's regulatory obligations. **The current local service has not demonstrated hosted 24/7 availability.** ([FINRA algorithmic-trading guidance](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-09), [Alpaca automated-trading risks](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/RisksAutoTrading.pdf))

## Honest data and bounded learning determine what paper results mean

Record source/feed, symbol identity, bar interval, adjustment mode, event/receive/decision times, revisions, and missing coverage. Use only completed observations known at decision time. Daily ETF freshness needs an exchange calendar; crypto needs an explicit UTC convention. Current validation rejects malformed, future, inconsistent, stale, and zero-volume bars. ETF daily bars permit a positive closed duration up to 24 hours to accommodate actual session endings and daylight-saving transitions; intraday ETF bars reject missing intervals within the same session. These checks do not establish full exchange-calendar coverage. Crypto providers can publish quote-derived zero-volume bars, so rejecting them is conservative filtering rather than proof the provider data is invalid. Fresh historical bars never substitute for a fresh executable quote. IEX signals and NBBO-based paper fills also represent different information sets. ([Alpaca crypto data](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/real-time-crypto-pricing-data), [Alpaca paper assumptions](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading))

Costs can defeat an apparently attractive signal. Alpaca's documented initial crypto tier is **15 basis points maker and 25 taker per side**; a marketable limit is a taker. Two taker sides therefore cost roughly 50 basis points before spread/slippage. Actual fees can arrive later and be deducted in the received asset; reconcile fee currency and credited quantity rather than trusting a cash-only synthetic model. Distinguish actual fees, reserves, funding, distributions, and operating spend. Until those activities reconcile, broker equity change is provisional performance, not accurate net profit. ([Alpaca crypto fees](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/crypto-fees))

Make learning a dataset and evaluation process first. Preserve holds, vetoes, expired proposals, failed orders, snapshots, policy versions, and subsequent marks at predefined horizons. A rejected proposal has observable later market prices, not an observed counterfactual fill. News and financial facts require first-available timestamps and revision history; today's restated filing cannot become yesterday's knowledge. Articles remain untrusted evidence with no execution permissions. ([SEC data contracts](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces))

Freeze candidate rules before forward testing. Train on earlier periods, evaluate later contiguous periods, purge overlapping outcome labels, and keep transformations inside training windows. Record every parameter trial. Ordinary time-series splitting helps preserve chronology but does not automatically fix financial label overlap; repeated searching can inflate the winning backtest. Compare candidates after costs with cash and an appropriate passive benchmark, reporting drawdown, exposure, turnover, tail loss, cost sensitivity, and independent trade episodes. There is no universal number of days or trades that proves profitability. Learners may propose candidates; they cannot raise risk limits, reset losing history, or promote themselves. ([TimeSeriesSplit](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html), [Backtest-overfitting research](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf), [Deflated Sharpe Ratio](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf))
